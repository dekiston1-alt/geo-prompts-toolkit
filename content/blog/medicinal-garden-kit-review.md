## My Experience: Week by Week

### Week One — Getting It in the Ground

I'll be honest: I was nervous planting the first seeds. I have a small raised bed on my back porch and a couple of deep containers on my balcony, so I split the seeds between both spaces to see what would thrive where.

The instructions were genuinely useful — specific enough that I felt guided, but not so dense that I felt overwhelmed. I'm not a gardener by trade. I've killed succulents before. So the fact that the materials held my hand without being condescending was a relief.

### Weeks Two and Three — Something Is Actually Happening

Tiny green sprouts showed up first from the chamomile and lemon balm. Then calendula started pushing through. I found myself checking on the plants every morning with my coffee, which sounds small but actually felt significant — I was building a ritual around something living and intentional.

By the end of week two I had six different varieties actively germinating. That felt like a win.

### A Month In — Real Results

By the end of week four, I had a handful of herbs visibly thriving. The lemon balm went from seed to lush little plant faster than I expected. I was able to harvest a few leaves to make a calming evening tea — and yes, I felt that. Not placebo. Just the quiet satisfaction of drinking something you grew with your own hands.

I also started my first small batch of calendula-infused oil, which I now use on the dry patches on my arms that used to bother me constantly. Within two weeks, my sister asked what I'd changed in my skincare routine.
