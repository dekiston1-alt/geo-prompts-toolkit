# How I Started Growing My Own Medicine (And Why I'll Never Go Back)

Two years ago, I was standing in the supplement aisle of a health food store trying to remember what I'd read about ashwagandha and whether I should add it to the small mountain of bottles already on my bathroom shelf. My monthly supplement spend had quietly crept past $150. I was tired all the time, scattered, and vaguely stressed about the cost of trying to feel better.

That moment didn't feel like a turning point. It just felt like Tuesday.

But looking back now, something in me was already starting to shift — a growing sense that what I was doing wasn't working, that I was chasing something I couldn't quite catch. I didn't know yet that the answer I was looking for was something I could grow with my own hands.

---

## The Burnout That Sent Me Looking for Something Different

I'd been running hard for a long time. Work, obligations, the particular exhaustion of always being on and available and productive. The kind of tired that sleep doesn't fully fix. I was doing "all the right things" on paper — taking supplements, exercising, trying to manage stress — and yet I felt fundamentally disconnected from my own body.

What I didn't recognize at the time was that I'd optimized my wellness into something that felt like another task on my to-do list. Another thing to manage and optimize and spend money on. It had stopped feeling nourishing a long time ago.

A friend who'd been growing a small herb garden mentioned offhand that she'd replaced her chamomile supplement with tea from plants she grew herself. She said it with a kind of ease that struck me. *I grew it myself.* Something about that phrase lodged in my chest and wouldn't leave.

---

## My First (Messy, Humbling) Attempts

I didn't start with a kit or a plan. I started with two pots, a bag of chamomile seeds from a grocery store, and a lot of overconfidence.

The first batch didn't do much. I watered too inconsistently, chose a spot with insufficient sun, and didn't really understand the germination process. By week three I had some sparse, leggy little sprouts that looked more confused than medicinal. I was frustrated, then embarrassed at my frustration — after all, it was just seeds.

I tried again with lemon balm. That went better, mostly because lemon balm is fairly determined to survive no matter what you do to it. I got some actual growth. I made a weak tea from the first small harvest and sat outside drinking it, feeling genuinely proud of something for the first time in months.

That small win mattered more than I expected it to.

### What Was Going Wrong

After a bit more research I realized my early failures came down to a few things: wrong soil, inconsistent watering, not understanding which herbs need more heat to germinate, and buying generic seeds with no guidance on medicinal varieties specifically. Most of what I found at regular garden stores was optimized for culinary use — flavor, yield, hardiness — not for therapeutic potency.

I needed better seeds and better guidance. That's when I started looking more seriously for a curated approach.

The difference wasn't magic. It was information and intention. Knowing the germination timelines for each plant meant I stopped giving up too early. ## The Transformation I Didn't See Coming

Here's the thing I couldn't have predicted: the gardening itself became part of the medicine.

Every morning I walk out to check on the plants. I notice what's blooming, what needs water, what's ready to harvest. It takes maybe ten minutes. Those ten minutes became the most grounding part of my day. Something about tending living things — being responsible for them, watching them respond to care — reconnected me to a slower, more patient way of being in the world.

I'd been so caught up in consuming wellness (buying, ordering, supplementing) that I'd forgotten the other version of it: *participating*. Actually doing something with your hands. Watching a cycle of growth unfold over weeks and feeling genuinely present for it.

My supplement spend dropped significantly. Not because I'd replaced everything I was taking — some things I still source elsewhere — but because I was building a foundation of plants I understood, grew, and trusted. Chamomile tea before bed instead of a melatonin gummy. Lemon balm in my water throughout the day instead of an expensive adaptogen blend. Calendula oil on my skin instead of a pricey natural moisturizer.

## Practical Tips for Beginners

If you're thinking about starting your own medicinal herb garden, here's what I'd tell my earlier self:

### Start with two or three plants, not ten

Overwhelm is the enemy of consistency. Pick a few herbs that match your actual wellness needs right now. Struggling with sleep? Chamomile and lemon balm. Want immune support? Echinacea and elderflower. Keep it small and focused until you build confidence.

### Get seeds that are selected for medicinal value

There's a meaningful difference between culinary herbs and medicinal ones, particularly in terms of the compounds that give them therapeutic value. Sourcing matters. [A kit specifically curated for medicinal growing](https://medicinalseedkit.com/kit/#aff=dekiston3a6d0) is worth the investment over random packets from a general garden center.

### Understand before you harvest

Part of what makes home herbalism so rewarding is knowing *when* and *how* to harvest each plant for maximum potency, and what to do with it once you have it. Read about each plant as you grow it. The learning is part of the joy.

### Give yourself permission to fail and try again

I killed things. I still occasionally kill things. Plants are patient teachers. They'll show you what they need if you're paying attention, and they'll forgive more mistakes than you expect if you keep showing up.

### Accept that this is a long game

A medicinal herb garden isn't a fast solution to anything. But that's actually the point. It teaches you to think in seasons rather than in instant results — and that shift in perspective carries over into the rest of your wellness life in ways that are hard to quantify but very easy to feel.

## Why I'll Never Go Back to How I Was Doing It Before

I'm not against supplements. I still take a few things I can't grow. But my relationship with my own health looks completely different now than it did two years ago.

And maybe more than anything — I feel like I'm in conversation with my own wellness instead of just managing it from the outside.

If you're feeling that same pull toward something more intentional, more connected, more yours — I'd encourage you to trust it. You can start small. You don't need a lot of space or expertise. You just need a few seeds and the willingness to show up for them.
